Missouri Permit Help

Getting your Missouri permit.

Before you can practice on public streets, Missouri requires an instruction permit. Here's the process, and we'll help you study for the test.

Laws change. These facts were checked against the Missouri Department of Revenue, but always confirm the current rules at the official links below before you go.

The process, step by step

1

Study, then pass three tests

Vision, road signs and written knowledge, at a Missouri State Highway Patrol (MSHP) driver examination station. The DOR does not give the tests.

2

Take your record to the DOR

Bring your Driver Examination Record to a Missouri DOR license office.

3

Bring your documents

Identity and lawful status, Social Security number, and proof of Missouri residency. A REAL ID permit needs two proofs of address.

4

Pay the fee

The Class F instruction permit costs $10.00 in total. It's valid for 12 months and renewable.

What's good to know

01 Who needs a permit

  • Any first-time driver without a valid license from another U.S. state, including adults 18 and older.
  • The minimum age is 15. There is no maximum age.
  • Applicants under 18 need a parent or guardian to co-sign.

02 What a permit lets you do

  • Always drive with a qualified licensed driver in the front passenger seat.
  • If you're 16 or older, that passenger must be at least 21.
  • Everyone in the car must wear a seat belt.

03 Adults 18+ (most learners)

  • Go straight from permit → practice → driving test at MSHP → license at the DOR.
  • Driver education is not required if you're 18 or older.
  • License validity: 18–20 → 3 years; 21–69 → 6 years.

04 Under 18 (GDL steps)

  • Hold the permit 182 days and log 40 supervised hours, including 10 at night.
  • Then an Intermediate License ($5, valid 2 years).
  • A 1–5 a.m. curfew and passenger limits apply.
