Missouri Permit Help

Getting your Missouri permit.

Before you can practice on real roads, Missouri requires an instruction permit. Here's exactly how to get one — we'll help you study for it too.

Laws change. These facts were checked against the Missouri Department of Revenue, but always confirm current rules at the official DOR links below before you go.

01 Who needs a permit

  • Any first-time driver without a valid license from another U.S. state needs a Missouri instruction permit — including adults 18 and older.
  • Minimum age is 15. There is no maximum age.
  • If the applicant is under 18, a parent or legal guardian must co-sign the application.

02 How to get it, step by step

  1. Study, then pass three tests — vision, road-sign recognition, and written knowledge — at a Missouri State Highway Patrol (MSHP) driver examination station. (The DOR does not give the tests.)
  2. Take your Driver Examination Record to a Missouri DOR license office.
  3. Bring proof documents: identity/lawful status, Social Security number, and Missouri residency. (A REAL ID permit needs two proofs of residency — see the DOR checklist.)
  4. Pay the fee. The Class F instruction permit total is $10.00 — valid 12 months and renewable.

Good news for adults: driver education is not required if you're 18 or older.

03 What a permit lets you do

  • You must always drive accompanied by a qualified licensed driver in the front passenger seat.
  • 16 or older: that passenger must be a licensed driver at least 21 years old.
  • Under 16: it must be a parent, grandparent, legal guardian, a qualified instructor, or a driver 25+ licensed 3+ years with written parental permission.
  • Everyone in the car must wear a seat belt.

04 Adults 18+ (most of our learners)

  • You can go straight from permit → practice → driving skills (road) test at MSHP, then apply and pay at the DOR for your full license.
  • License validity: ages 18–20 → 3 years; ages 21–69 → 6 years.

Drivers under 18 go through extra Graduated Driver License (GDL) steps: hold the permit 182 days, log 40 supervised hours (10 at night), then an Intermediate License ($5, valid 2 years) with a 1–5 a.m. curfew and passenger limits.
